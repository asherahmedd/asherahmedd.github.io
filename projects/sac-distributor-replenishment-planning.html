BUSINESS CASE

From distributor demand to controlled replenishment.

Rather than treating primary sales as consumption, the application uses secondary sell-out to estimate downstream demand, combines distributor stock and confirmed inbound supply, then identifies when and how much to replenish.

  • Automated proposals or planner-entered overrides
  • Allocation simulation against supply constraints
  • Workflow-based review and approval
  • Released request for warehouse execution
  • Feedback to prevent repeat allocation
Key boundary: SAC creates and governs the plan. The ERP/WMS or fulfillment team executes the approved replenishment.
01 / PROCESS ARCHITECTURE

End-to-end replenishment flow.

Separating planning calculations, approval, and warehouse execution avoids treating an unapproved plan as a delivery instruction.

01 · ActualsPrimary sales, secondary sell-out, distributor stock and open deliveries
02 · Planning ModelTime, distributor, material, stock, demand and version
03 · Gap CalculationProjected inventory, safety stock, lead time, demand coverage
04 · AllocationScheduled data action or manual planning input and override
05 · ApprovalSAC Calendar review, publish / release gate and lock
06 · ExecutionApproved output to ERP/WMS; warehouse confirms dispatch
Feedback loop: dispatch, goods issue, delivery and distributor stock updates return as actuals in the next planning cycle. Only confirmed inbound supply should be included in the pipeline calculation.
02 / DECISION LOGIC

Replenish at the right quantity and time.

Critical metrics and controls used to translate demand signals into an operational allocation proposal.

01

Demand velocity

Daily sell-out, trailing averages, seasonality and promotional uplifts.

02

Projected stock

Current stock + confirmed arrivals − forecast consumption.

03

Reorder point

Expected lead-time demand + safety stock buffer.

04

Allocation constraints

Available central stock, case pack/MOQ, capacity and priorities.

Illustrative calculation logic

Projected Stock (at receipt) = Current On-hand + Confirmed Inbound before receipt − Expected Sell-out until receipt

Target Stock = Demand over target cover period + Safety Stock

Net Proposal = MAX(0, Target Stock − Projected Stock at receipt)

Released Qty = MIN(Net Proposal, Allocatable Supply) [rounded to pack/MOQ rules]

If stock is projected to fall below the reorder threshold before replenishment arrives, the planner should flag an exception and advance the delivery or escalate constrained supply. The formulas are business illustrations, not SAP-delivered calculations.

Preventing false replenishment

  • Primary sales represent sell-in and distributor supply, not necessarily consumer demand.
  • Secondary sales are the demand signal; correct for late or incomplete distributor reporting.
  • Open pipeline includes confirmed inbound quantities and dates, excluding already received supply.
  • Stock snapshot should account for sellable vs. blocked/expired stock.
  • Approval and dispatch IDs prevent allocating the same request twice.
  • Units and master data must align by material, distributor and sales unit.
03 / APPLICATION EXPERIENCE

Two ways to build a replenishment proposal.

One governed planning model supports automation and planner control, with approvals before release.

AUTOMATED PATH

Scheduled replenishment run

Run a parameterized data action to compute proposal quantities and priority classifications. SAP Calendar can schedule recurring data-action tasks. The result stays in a planning version for review.

MANUAL PATH

Custom allocation form

Planners enter or adjust SKU-by-distributor quantities in an input-enabled table, or run a tailored data action with selected parameters. Overridden quantities stay traceable in the planning version.

CONTROLLED RELEASE

Review → approve → execute

Calendar reviewers assess the proposal. Approved data is published/released, then mapped and sent through a separately designed integration to the downstream order or warehouse process.

05 / ENGINEERING IMPLEMENTATION

Planning architecture and delivery controls.

Planning-ready data foundation

Integrate distributor sell-out, sell-in, on-hand and delivery schedules; align calendar, customer, material and unit mappings. Import into an SAC planning model or use supported Datasphere-integrated planning storage.

Custom data actions

Use parameters, advanced formulas, allocations and conditional planning rules for stock coverage, reorder proposals, pack rounding and central stock limits.

Versioned approval

Use draft / submitted / approved states with Calendar review, planner-to-approver assignments and data locking or publication after acceptance. Prevent edits to released quantities.

Warehouse execution handoff

Map approved distributor/material/date/quantity records to an ERP/WMS service through integration middleware, API, or governed export. Track receipt/dispatch confirmation and failures.

TECHNICAL VALIDATION

What SAC supports natively — and what needs integration.

SAP Analytics Cloud supports input-enabled planning models, data actions (including advanced formulas and allocations), scheduled automated data actions, and Calendar tasks with review and approval. The custom allocation interface can be built with optimized stories or analytic applications and scripting. The final conversion of an approved replenishment plan into a warehouse order, outbound delivery or stock transfer is not inherently performed by a data action: it requires a defined downstream interface, compatible business objects and appropriate operational authorizations.
